01

Two ways to move forward.

Choose the type that matches your question. A single screen or a whole flow is the context you review; the decision type describes the outcome you need.

Already seeing Prioritization? Choice is available too.

Each flow has one saved decision, so an existing flow opens the type you previously selected. To try the other type, open Properties → Reset local decision → confirm, then choose Start choice or Start prioritization.

Reset removes that flow’s decision, ballots, and previous attempts from this browser. It also restores the original flow if you applied a chosen version. To keep an existing decision, try the other type on another flow instead.

Available now

Choice

“Which version should move forward?”

Compare exactly two flow versions. Each participant chooses one version or abstains. The decision maker records the final choice and rationale.

Use it for: a current checkout versus a proposed checkout.

Follow the Choice walkthrough →
Available now

Prioritization

“What should we improve first?”

Collect improvements to a flow. Participants distribute a vote allowance across proposals. The decision maker sets a final order and quarterly commitment cutoff.

Use it for: planning next quarter’s onboarding improvements.

Approval—for example, deciding whether a flow is ready to ship—is a future type. It is not selectable in this version.

02

Your first decision.

Start from any flow in the dashboard or the interactive flow on the home page. No connector setup is needed to try the examples.

  1. Open a flow, then select Decision.

    The scales icon sits immediately before Reviews in the flow toolbar. It opens a wider side panel. On desktop, drag the divider to resize it.

  2. Select a decision type.

    Use Start choice for your own question, or Load choice demo example for a prepared alternative. Prioritization has the same two starting actions and begins with simulated specialist proposals.

  3. Explore the four sections.

    Set your question in Properties, read the automatically published evidence brief, then review the options in Deliberate. A demo example opens Evidence first.

  4. Start round one when preparation is complete.

    The footer explains missing requirements. Add a question, keep at least one decision maker, and prepare two named versions or at least two named proposals.

Question, people, dates, and voting rules.

03

Explore before you decide.

The flow viewer is the shared context for the conversation. These tools are available alongside Decision.

Compare appsAdd another available flow to the comparison canvas. Comparing apps does not create the A/B versions of a Choice decision; prepare those inside Decision.
PrototypeSwitch to the prototype presentation view. Use it to inspect the journey in context.

Decision notes stay with the option or snapshot they describe. Applying a chosen version does not move the original flow’s comments onto different screens.

04

Set the parameters.

In Properties, click an editable value to change it. Press Enter or leave the field to save; Escape cancels a text edit. Parameters lock when voting starts.

PropertyHow it works
Decision questionState what the team is deciding. Required before round one.
Due dateRecord when the decision is needed. Optional; it does not automatically close voting.
Decision makersChoose who manages rounds and records the outcome. At least one must remain assigned. Either assigned decision maker can finalize.
Input providersChoose the other people who can propose and vote. Someone in both roles receives one ballot.
People affectedDescribe the people or teams affected. This text does not invite users or grant voting access.
Decision typeChoice or Prioritization, selected during setup. Reset the local decision to select a different type.
Commitment (days)Record how long the team intends to commit to the decision. Optional.
Earliest revisitSet a date explicitly, or use the suggested due date plus commitment duration. No reminder is sent.
Votes per personChoice is fixed at one. Prioritization defaults to five; a decision maker can set a positive whole-number allowance during preparation.

Viewing as lets you switch between the four simulated team members. You start as You · Decision maker. Agents contribute information and suggestions; they do not vote.

05

Start with shared evidence.

The Product Data agent automatically publishes a brief when you create a decision. In this prototype, all connector reports and agent findings are illustrative examples.

Journey analytics

Amplitude · simulated

Example funnel completion, drop-off, completion time, and differences between cohorts.

Business context

Business metrics · simulated

Illustrative conversion, support contacts, and handling cost. No revenue uplift or savings is validated.

Customer understanding

Research · simulated

Example task results, interview themes, quotes, sample sizes, and limitations.

Specialist inputs

Research, Writing, CX & UI

Mocked observations and recommendations, consolidated into improvement proposals with attribution.

  • Follow the sources. Summary references link to supporting evidence. Use Open local source report to inspect the period, sample, observation, interpretation, and recommendation.
  • Read the gaps. Missing retention data, small samples, and conflicting findings are part of the brief.
  • Refresh before voting. Refresh demo evidence publishes another example brief during preparation.
  • Keep a common basis for voting. Starting round one freezes the brief for both rounds.

What if new information arrives?

Simulate new finding adds a report outside the frozen brief. Read it first. A decision maker can choose Incorporate and restart voting, confirm, and prepare a new attempt. Previous results stay in history.

06

Choose between two versions.

Use Choice when two concrete alternatives need a decision. A and B become frozen snapshots when round one starts.

  1. Prepare A and B in Deliberate.

    A starts from the current flow; B starts as a copy. The demo example adds a visibly different introduction and confirmation. Both versions can be edited during preparation.

  2. Open Edit version and screens.

    Name each version, describe the change, edit screen titles, move screens up or down, add/remove screens, and upload replacement PNG, JPEG, or WebP images under 10 MB each. Keep at least one screen in each version.

  3. Start round one to freeze the comparison.

    Versions, screen order, images, evidence, and the voting roster are fixed for the attempt. Later changes to the original flow do not alter these snapshots.

  4. Open both snapshots as each participant.

    Use Open version or the canvas’s Version A / Version B controls. Each participant must open both before submitting a ballot. “Opened” records access, not proof of a completed review.

  5. Vote, discuss, and reconsider.

    Select A, B, or Abstain in each round. Add optional feedback in Discussion, with a version and optional screen reference. Follow the shared voting process below.

On a phone: opening a version shows a readable canvas preview. Select Back to Decision to return to the panel.

07

Plan what to improve next.

Use Prioritization when the team has a flow and several ideas for improving it next quarter. The proposals are improvements, not alternative flow versions.

  1. Inspect the journey and suggestions.

    Open the journey context. Review the consolidated Research, Writing, CX, and UI proposals. Attribution shows the contributors; evidence links explain the supporting findings.

  2. Shape the proposal list together.

    Assigned participants can add improvements during preparation. Decision makers can edit proposals, link evidence and affected screens, record expected benefit and rough effort, remove duplicates, or merge proposals while preserving their attribution and evidence.

  3. Lock the list by starting round one.

    Keep at least two named proposals. Voting freezes the list and a snapshot of the journey so everyone considers the same context.

  4. Distribute the vote allowance.

    Each participant gets five votes by default. Put several votes on one proposal or spread them across options. Whole numbers only; the total cannot exceed the allowance. Leaving some votes unused is allowed. Abstention is a separate choice.

  5. Set a final order and quarterly cutoff.

    After both rounds, the decision maker uses the ranking arrows in Decide. Choose how many top improvements are committed this quarter; the rest remain later candidates. Record the rationale, especially when the final order differs from vote totals.

A ranking becomes a commitment at the cutoff.

The cutoff separates Committed this quarter from Later candidates. Effort is a rough estimate; the prototype does not calculate team capacity or schedule the work.

08

Two rounds. One informed call.

The same process supports both types. Votes guide the discussion; the decision maker owns the final outcome.

Round 01

Vote independently.

Use Viewing as to test each eligible person. Submit a ballot; you can update it while the round is open. Other ballots and totals stay hidden.

Discussion

Share the reasoning.

The decision maker closes round one and reveals results. Add option-specific pros, cons, and comments. Inspect evidence before reconsidering.

Round 02

Vote again.

The decision maker opens a fresh round with empty ballots. Round-one results remain visible; current ballots stay hidden until round two closes.

  • Who votes: decision makers and input providers, with one allowance per person even if they have both roles.
  • Who manages rounds: decision makers only. Switch back to You to manage the seeded demo.
  • Missing ballots: closing early requires confirmation. A missing ballot is not counted as an abstention.
  • Ties: no automatic winner is chosen. A tie does not prevent the decision maker from recording an outcome after both rounds close.

Blind voting is an interaction simulation on this device. The participant switcher is not authentication or a private multi-user ballot system.

09

Make the next step explicit.

After round two closes, open Decide. Only a decision maker can finalize, and a rationale is required.

ActionResult
Finalize ChoiceRecord the chosen snapshot, rationale, decision maker, and time. It does not automatically replace the current flow.
Make current versionA separate, confirmed Choice action. Applies the chosen snapshot to the dashboard and viewer in this browser. Use Restore original version to undo that local override.
Finalize PrioritizationRecord an ordered list and a cutoff of at least one committed proposal. It does not automatically create or schedule tasks.
Request changes insteadRecord why the options need revision without choosing a winner. The decision remains unresolved.
Reopen decisionConfirm a new preparation attempt. The previous attempt stays in history; ballots, opened checks, and discussion start fresh. Edit the options, then run voting again.
Communicated on / toOptional fields documenting when and where the outcome was communicated. No email, Slack message, or notification is sent.

To replace a frozen snapshot, change the voting roster, or revise the locked proposal list, reopen the decision first. These changes require a new voting attempt.

11

A few useful answers.

Why can’t I change Prioritization to Choice directly?
A flow keeps one decision record, including its options, ballots, and history. The type is selected at creation. Use Properties → Reset local decision to start a different type, or use another flow to preserve the current decision. Reopen decision starts another attempt of the same type.
Why is Start round one disabled?
Read the requirement message in the panel footer. Add a decision question, retain a decision maker, and prepare two named nonempty versions for Choice or at least two named proposals for Prioritization. Check that you are viewing as a decision maker.
Why can’t I submit a Choice ballot?
As the selected participant, open both frozen versions first, then choose A, B, or Abstain. Opening a version as You does not mark it opened for Sara, Marcus, or Nina.
Why can’t I edit a version or proposal?
Editing is available to decision makers during preparation. Options, evidence, and participants lock when voting starts. Reopen the decision to prepare a new attempt; the previous attempt remains in history.
Does the option with the most votes always win?
No. Voting is advisory. The decision maker can choose a different version or final ranking and must record a rationale. This is also how ties are resolved.
Will colleagues see the decision I just created?
No. Decisions, uploaded snapshot images, and your choice of current version stay in this browser. A different browser, device, or site address has its own state. Clearing site data can remove your local work. Loading a demo here does not create a shared team decision.
Can I trust the numbers as actual product results?
No. Decision’s reports, quotes, metrics, and specialist findings are visibly labeled demo or simulated. They illustrate how evidence supports a decision; they are not measured results from your product.
Does this guide change or reset my decisions?
No. This page is read-only documentation. Its walkthrough links move around the guide; Open Alignment returns to the app. Resetting happens only through the confirmed action inside a flow’s Decision panel.
